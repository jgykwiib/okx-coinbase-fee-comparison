## Security: what each side can actually prove

Both are custodial accounts. Neither gives you a bank deposit guarantee on crypto, and neither insurance policy covers every way you can lose money.

### OKX: reserve reports plus a US legal record

OKX publishes monthly proof-of-reserves reports verified by Hacken. The 45th report arrived in August 2026. That is a stronger signal than a private company’s word, but it shows assets, not a full audit of liabilities. The disclosure combines customer and company digital assets across global entities and does not provide a separate US entity total. The inclusion tool lets customers check their balance, but it is not a guarantee of future withdrawals.

In February 2025, OKX’s international entity pleaded guilty in the US to running an unlicensed money-transmitting business and agreed to pay about $504 million. A compliance consultant obligation runs to February 2027. That is a real compliance cost, and it belongs in the decision.

### Coinbase: public filings plus a data incident

Coinbase is listed on Nasdaq, files audited financials with the SEC, and joined the S&P 500 in May 2025. It says the large majority of customer crypto sits in cold storage. Its crime insurance covers a breach of Coinbase’s systems, not a compromised personal account. For cash, eligible pass-through balances can be insured up to $250,000. Crypto is not covered by that.

In May 2025, Coinbase disclosed an insider attack in which support personnel collected customer data used in social-engineering scams. It offered reimbursement to customers tricked into sending funds. The incident is a reminder that account data and support workflows are part of the security model.

Both exchanges have had incidents. The useful question is what each one discloses and what it covers after something goes wrong.
